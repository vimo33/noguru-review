Academy · Capital & exposure

Changing exposure deliberately

Reducing future orders and selling existing holdings are different actions.

← All lessons

Decide what you mean by “smaller”

Lowering future order sizes changes new exposure. Cancelling an unfilled buy can release its reserved cash. Selling an existing holding reduces that holding but may realise a loss and incur fees. Pausing a bot does not automatically do all three.

Write a review plan before the pressure arrives

The original lesson illustrates an if/then plan written while calm. The table below preserves its example responses. It is a generic teaching template: the thresholds are deliberately unspecified, and none of these fractions is a recommended NoGuru configuration.

The source’s illustrative precommitted plan
Event defined in advanceResponse in the source example
Drawdown passes a chosen review lineHalve the risk fraction
A chosen consecutive-loss count is reachedHalve the fraction again
The method is new, changed, or outside its tested conditionsStart at half size or less
The impulse is to increase size to recover a lossUse the smallest size, or stop for the day

The transferable exercise is to write the trigger, the information to review and the possible response before seeing the result. A review can lead to stopping. It is not a commitment to keep trading, and a prewritten plan does not remove the need to consider current circumstances.

The source’s 20% drawdown-budget arithmetic

Under exact fractional losses with no costs, a 1% loss per trade leaves 80.16% of starting equity after 22 losses; the 23rd takes it below 80%. At 0.5%, 44 losses leave 80.21%; the 45th crosses that line. This is the source’s hypothetical 20% budget, not a loss allowance recommended to a reader.

Halving a position halves its money exposure for the same price move under a simple proportional model. It also halves gains on that move. Over repeated losses the balance compounds, so the cumulative change is not a straight line. Reducing or closing actual holdings can involve fees and realised losses; it does not repair earlier losses.

Avoid a recovery deadline

Adding money to recover a past loss creates more exposure; it does not make recovery due. A decision about size should account for money already tied up, remaining obligations, fees and the possibility that the asset never recovers.

Keep an operational record

When changing settings, record what was changed, which existing orders remain, and whether trade management continues. Verify the actual state at the exchange as well as in the automation interface.

How this relates to NoGuru

Changing NoGuru or Cornix settings can alter execution and make your results differ from the public model. See the stopping guidance before changing a running setup.
