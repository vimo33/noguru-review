Position size and planned risk

The amount committed to an order and the amount at risk are different ideas.

← All lessons

A stop-based example

Suppose a hypothetical method defines a $20 planned loss and a stop 5% below entry. Ignoring fees and execution gaps, $20 ÷ 0.05 implies a $400 position. This is an explanation of stop-based sizing, not a recommendation to use those figures.

Why the assumptions matter

A stop order is an execution instruction, not a guaranteed maximum loss. Gaps, slippage, outages and available liquidity can produce a worse fill. Without a stop, this formula cannot be used to claim that the position risks only $20.

Look beyond the first buy

Several additional buys can commit much more money than the opening order. Identify the total planned allocation, reserves, concurrent positions and exchange minimums before interpreting a small first order.

How this relates to NoGuru

NoGuru intentionally has no stop-loss. The generic 1–2% stop-risk idea in older educational material is not its strategy. The amount allocated can suffer substantial loss.
