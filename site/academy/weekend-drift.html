Academy · Market fundamentals

Weekend moves and comparison groups

A high revisit rate can be less informative than it first appears.

← All lessons

Ask “compared with what?”

The original NoGuru lesson describes the operator’s own study of hourly BTC, ETH and SOL prices from January 2024. It compared weekend moves with weekday windows and then examined controls of similar move size. That is a source study, not an invented example. Its transferable question is whether the calendar explains a result after the size of the move is considered.

A short move starts closer to its reference price than a large one. A raw revisit comparison can therefore mix the effect of distance with the effect of the weekend. Matching on size is a way to test that alternative explanation; the matching choices themselves need scrutiny.

Make the comparison reproducible

Questions to answer before interpreting a revisit rate
DefineWhy it matters
Data and event windowName the venue, pair, dates, timezone, missing-data treatment and exact start/end of each move
Reference and outcomeSpecify the price to revisit, the permitted distance and a fixed observation horizon
Comparison windowsUse the same duration on other days; disclose overlapping windows and reused observations
Size matchingCompare similar moves and show whether changing the matching rule changes the conclusion
Counts and unresolved casesShow the event count, matched-control count and moves that did not revisit within the horizon
Trading interpretationA revisit is not an executable trade: entries, exits, fees and available liquidity still need a separate test
Source-study status and the omitted figures

The source preview gives coin-level move figures, raw revisit percentages and a matching-sensitivity range. The saved preview does not supply the underlying price series, complete window definitions, event counts or calculation code needed to reproduce them. Those empirical figures are deferred here, not relabeled as fictional or treated as disproved.

The useful lesson is the comparison design: define the event, match plausible alternative explanations, and disclose sensitivity to the choice of controls. The original lesson remains linked below for its dated account of the research; no new estimates have been invented to replace it.

Define “eventually”

A price revisit after two hours and one after two years are different experiences for committed capital. The observation window, open cases and costs must be visible. An eventual revisit does not establish a tradeable profit or an acceptable wait.

Session rules change

Historical CME weekend gaps arose when a futures session was closed while spot traded. Current hours and maintenance windows must be checked with the venue; old gap folklore should not be treated as a current trading rule.

How this relates to NoGuru

NoGuru does not promise that a gap fills, that a coin recovers or that capital becomes available within a chosen period.
