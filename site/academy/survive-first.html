Compounding includes the losses

Returns multiply; they do not simply add up.

The order of the arithmetic

With no deposits or withdrawals, +20% followed by −20% turns an illustrative $1,000 into $1,200 and then $960. The average of the two percentages is zero, but the money is 4% below its starting value.

Two invented years, compared

The original lesson compares two hypothetical return paths. Both start at an index of 100, with no deposits, withdrawals or costs. These monthly returns are invented for arithmetic; they are not targets, forecasts or NoGuru results.

What multiplying by zero means

If the balance reaches zero, multiplying it by any later finite return still gives zero. For example, 0 × 1.50 = 0. Adding new money would change the starting capital; it would not recover the old account through compounding. A partial loss and a complete loss therefore have different arithmetic consequences.

Exposure is the practical question

Compounding can amplify gains and losses. Retaining proceeds in a trading account leaves them exposed to later trades. Moving some funds elsewhere changes the amount available for orders; neither choice guarantees a better outcome.

A useful review

Track cash, the current market value of holdings, trading costs, subscriptions and money added or removed. Compare like-for-like periods and distinguish realised sales from unsold holdings.

How this relates to NoGuru

NoGuru’s public setup can retain proceeds for later trading. This is not automatic Bitcoin conversion, guaranteed growth or protection of previous gains.
