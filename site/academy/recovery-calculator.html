Academy · Interactive arithmetic

What does recovery take?

The gain needed after a loss. A calculation of the hurdle, never a prediction that an asset will clear it.

42.86% gain needed

An illustrative $1,000 becomes $700.00 after the loss. Returning to $1,000 requires a $300.00 gain.

The formula

Required gain = loss ÷ (1 − loss), with percentages expressed as decimals.

For 30%: 0.30 ÷ 0.70 = 0.4286, or 42.86%.

The gain is a hurdle, not a forecast.

The basic calculation assumes no future return. It cannot tell you whether recovery happens or whether to hold. At a 100% loss, no percentage gain on the zero balance can restore it.

Fees, deposits and withdrawals are excluded. $1,000 is an arithmetic example, not a suggested allocation.

Optional: choose a hypothetical monthly pace

This restores the original tool’s sensitivity exercise. Enter your own constant monthly pace; no default is suggested and no rate is claimed achievable. Real results do not arrive as steady monthly gains.

Enter a hypothetical pace to calculate the number of whole monthly periods.

For a loss fraction d and positive monthly pace r, periods = log(1 ÷ (1 − d)) ÷ log(1 + r), rounded up to whole periods. Fees, deposits, withdrawals and changing returns are excluded. This is arithmetic from your assumptions, not a NoGuru projection or a reason to keep holding.

For NoGuru, the open loss still matters.

No stop-loss is used. Further buys can lower average cost while increasing committed capital. They cannot guarantee recovery.
