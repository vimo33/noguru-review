Small samples can mislead

A good-looking short run does not establish a lasting advantage.

← All lessons

An invented probability example

Imagine independent trades with a 40% chance of gaining $200 and a 60% chance of losing $100. The arithmetic average is +$20 before costs. Yet a run of ten trades can still lose money. These probabilities are invented; they do not describe NoGuru.

Five short runs can tell different stories

The original lesson displays five simulated ten-trade stretches from this invented model. Using R = $100 only as a fixed payoff unit, each win adds 2R and each loss subtracts 1R. These are examples from the source, not five NoGuru observations or a new simulation run.

Those five totals average +2R, but any one looks very different from that average. Selecting only the best window would hide much of the model’s variation.

An exact calculation under invented assumptions

Assume exactly ten independent trials, an unchanged 40% chance of a win, fixed +2R/−1R outcomes and no costs. If K is the number of wins, the total is 2K − (10 − K) = 3K − 10. A negative result means K is 0, 1, 2 or 3.

Adding those binomial probabilities gives 38.2281%, or 38.2% rounded. The model has a positive arithmetic expectation and still loses over many ten-trade windows. This probability describes the invented model only; it is not an estimate of NoGuru’s chance of losing.

See every possible ten-trade result

For each K, probability = C(10, K) × 0.4K × 0.610−K. This exact distribution replaces a sampled histogram; there is no claim to reproduce the source’s 2,000 simulation draws.

More rows are not the whole answer

Trades exposed to the same market move may not be independent. A thousand entries from one market regime are not the same as evidence across many conditions. Sample size, selection, dependence, costs and open losses all matter.

Evaluate the trial for its real purpose

A short trial can answer whether you understand the workflow, can follow orders and know how to stop. A profitable fortnight cannot validate long-term performance, and an unfinished cycle cannot establish its eventual duration.

How this relates to NoGuru

Do not infer an expected return, breakeven period or recommended capital from the 14-day trial or the completed-cycle count.
