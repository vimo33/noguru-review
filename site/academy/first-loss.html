Academy · Evaluation & habits

When a loss becomes real

A loss deserves attention without becoming a promise of a comeback.

← All lessons

Check the facts first

Identify whether the loss is realised through a sale or still in an open holding. Check the fills, fees, settings and account balances. An execution problem and an adverse market move require different explanations.

One result has limits

One loss alone cannot establish how a strategy will perform over time. Equally, calling a loss “normal” is not proof that a strategy works. Look for evidence rather than pressure to keep trading or increase the amount at risk.

The source’s invented bad-start arithmetic

Use the same hypothetical independent model as the sample-size lesson: 40% wins at +2R and 60% losses at −1R, unchanged between trials and before costs. The probability of starting with n losses is 0.6n.

Invented model only · not NoGuru loss probabilities
StartCalculationProbability
First trade loses0.660%
First three lose0.6³21.6%
First five lose0.6⁵7.8%, rounded

This shows that a positive expectation in an invented model can coexist with a bad start. It does not show that an actual losing strategy has an edge, or that another trade is owed a win.

Write the review criteria in advance

The original lesson proposes a falsification rule: state what would count against the approach before a result makes the answer emotionally convenient. A useful written exercise separates evidence about the method from personal reasons to reduce exposure or stop.

Pre-outcome review prompts · fill in your own circumstances, not a prescribed threshold
Record in advanceQuestion at review
The rule/version and claim being testedAm I evaluating the same method I started with?
Required operating factsDid orders, account permissions and balances behave as expected?
The evidence that would challenge the claimWhat observation would change my view, including after a profitable result?
Review point and information neededIs the evidence adequate, comparable and inclusive of open losses and costs?
Reasons to pause or stop independently of the testHave my circumstances, exposure or the service changed?

A prewritten review does not oblige you to wait, add money or accept a loss threshold. You can stop before the planned review. Nor does meeting a minimum sample size establish safety or profitability.

Stopping is a legitimate choice

You do not owe a system more capital or time because you already started. Understand the operational consequences of pausing new orders, ending automation and dealing with existing holdings. Selling may realise a loss; holding may deepen it.

How this relates to NoGuru

NoGuru does not guarantee recovery and has no stop-loss. A position can remain below cost for months or fail to recover altogether.
