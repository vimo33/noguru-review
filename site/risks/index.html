Risks, said plainly

Built to avoid forced selling. Here is what can still go wrong.

The bot buys real coins with your own money, never borrows, and is built to sell a coin only above its average cost. That removes the usual ways traders are forced out at a loss. These are the risks that remain.

It never sells at a loss.

Nothing is borrowed, so nothing can be force-sold. The bot buys a coin in steps and sells only once the price is above the average of what it paid, so a finished cycle is never a loss. One detail in the record: Cornix allows at most ten buys in one trade, so a longer cycle is split into several Cornix trades. One trade of such a group can show a loss on its own; the group as a whole, which is the cycle, never does.

The risk sits in the trades that are still open. While a coin is below its average cost, it is worth less than you paid for it, and that can last a long time. So only put in money you can leave alone for months, and never money you need for bills.

When prices keep falling.

The bot buys more of a coin as it gets cheaper, up to a fixed limit per coin and a fixed total across all coins. After that it waits. It does not sell in a panic, and there is no stop loss.

Most trades finish within a day. A few take weeks or months, and the record shows those too. The bot trades established, large coins, so a coin failing outright or being removed from an exchange is unlikely. It is not impossible, and a coin that never recovers would be a real loss.

In a broad market fall several coins can be below cost at the same time. When the cash set aside for the bot is used up, buying pauses until a sale frees some.

The signals, and what NoGuru cannot control.

NoGuru’s signals have been delivered without an outage since the record began on 1 August 2026. If there is ever an extended outage on NoGuru’s side, the lost time is credited to your subscription.

Between a signal and a finished order there are two more services: Cornix and your exchange. NoGuru does not control them or your account settings. An order can arrive late, be rejected or fill only in part, and your own results depend on your settings, your start date and the cash in the account.

It is worth opening Cornix from time to time to check that orders are going through. Three things to look at: your open trades, the cash still free, and any alerts Cornix shows.

How the three roles fit together

Your exchange and your key.

Your money stays at your exchange, in your own name, so the exchange’s security and solvency matter. Use one you trust.

Cornix trades with a key you create. Allow spot trading only and keep withdrawals switched off: the key can then trade but never move money out. Keep the bot’s money in a sub-account of its own, and never paste the key anywhere but Cornix.

What comes off the result.

The record is shown before fees. Three costs come off your own result: your exchange’s trading fee on every buy and every sale, the NoGuru subscription, and the Cornix plan.

The two subscriptions are fixed amounts, so they weigh more on a small account. Below about 500 USDT the bot does not work; at about 2,000 USDT it can fill part of its orders; above 3,000 USDT it can fill most of them. If you want to stop.

Stop the bot in Cornix. From then on it places no new orders. Stopping does not sell anything: the coins you hold stay in your exchange account, and you decide whether to keep them or sell them.

Billing is separate. NoGuru is renewed by invoice, so to end it you simply don’t pay the next one. A Cornix plan is managed in Cornix.
